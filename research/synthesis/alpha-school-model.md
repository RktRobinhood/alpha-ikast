# Alpha School model: claim inventory

Status: synthesis of supplied research; claims are not yet independently verified.

## Central claim

Alpha School appears to use a proprietary orchestration layer, TimeBack, over multiple specialist learning applications rather than relying on one general-purpose “AI teacher.”

## Reported operating loop

```text
placement/assessment data
    -> TimeBack learner pathway
    -> specialist learning application
    -> activity and mastery evidence returned to TimeBack
    -> progress, goals, XP, and pacing views
    -> guide intervention when needed
```

## Reported roles and experiences

### Student

- Sees daily work, progress, goals, XP, and an individualized pathway.
- Moves among subject-specific applications.
- Receives visible feedback about pace and mastery.

### Guide

- Focuses on motivation, coaching, relationships, and intervention.
- Reportedly has access to student progress information.
- Is not simply replaced by tutoring software.

### Parent

- Reportedly can see progress information.

## Reported schedule philosophy

The supplied research describes roughly two hours of core academic work, followed by workshops, projects, entrepreneurship, speaking, physical activity, and other experiences. This is a pedagogical and organizational claim distinct from the software architecture.

## Potentially reusable ideas

- A shared orchestration layer can unify heterogeneous subject tools.
- Placement and learning evidence can drive personalized pathways.
- Students benefit from a legible view of goals, progress, and next actions.
- Teachers need an intervention-oriented view, not merely activity logs.
- The platform can coordinate content without owning all content delivery.

## Questions that screen-level research should answer

- How are daily goals generated and changed?
- What does the student choose, and what is assigned?
- How are mastery thresholds defined and overridden?
- How are external-tool events matched to curriculum competencies?
- What happens when data is missing, contradictory, or stale?
- Which signals create a guide intervention?
- How are pacing and incentives designed without encouraging shallow point chasing?
- What can parents see, and how is that access governed?
- What traditional, standardized, mastery, or performance assessments does Alpha actually use, and how do they affect progression?
